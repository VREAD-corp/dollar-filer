3. Exchange rate (naira per unit)
Where these rates come from

The figures are the "central rate" the Central Bank of Nigeria publishes every working day on its exchange rates page. A script copies them once a day; the date of the last copy is shown here once loaded. For USD the central rate sits within ₦1 of the NFEM rate that CBN calls "the official exchange rate for the day".

The Act says foreign-currency expenses are converted "at the official exchange rate published by the Central Bank of Nigeria for the relevant date or period" (s.20(4)). It has no line as clear for income, so the safest record is the CBN rate on each day you were paid. The year average is a shortcut for planning. The rate your bank or Grey gave you is not the official rate.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Capital gains, including crypto disposals (the Act taxes them, s.4(1)(j) and Part VIII; we left them out of version 2026.1).
  • Companies, partnerships and the small-company 0% rate. This is for individuals only.
  • Presumptive tax for people without records (s.29): the rules come from regulations not yet in our hands.
  • Non-residents. If you live abroad, money you send home is not taxed just for arriving in Nigeria (s.4; PFPTRC "Fact not fear #2").

Do you need to file a return?

If you lived in Nigeria in 2026 and earned income, the answer is almost always yes, even when the tax comes to zero: "every taxable person whether or not liable to pay tax" files (NTAA s.13(1)). Our reading of the deadline for 2026 income is 31 March 2027.
